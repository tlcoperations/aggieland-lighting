In Bryan–College Station, a ceiling fan runs most of the year. Getting the size right makes the difference between a room that feels comfortable and a fan that just spins.

## Fan size by room size

Fan size is measured blade tip to blade tip. Measure the room's square footage (length × width) and start here:

| Room size | Example rooms | Fan size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 75 sq ft | Small bath, laundry, walk-in closet | 29"–36" |
| 76–144 sq ft | Bedroom, home office | 36"–44" |
| 145–225 sq ft | Primary bedroom, kitchen, dining | 44"–52" |
| 226–400 sq ft | Living room, game room | 52"–60" |
| Over 400 sq ft | Open-concept living, large patio | 60"+ or two fans |

In long or open rooms, two fans spaced evenly often beat one oversized fan.

## How high it should hang

- The blades should sit about **7 to 9 feet above the floor** — never lower than 7 feet.
- With **8-foot ceilings**, choose a flush-mount or "hugger" fan.
- With **9-foot ceilings and up**, use a downrod to bring the fan down into the room. A rough guide: a 6" downrod for 9-foot ceilings, 12" for 10-foot, 18" for 11-foot, and longer from there.
- Keep blades at least **18" from walls** and clear of light fixtures and cabinets.

## Patios and porches: damp vs. wet rated

- **Damp-rated** fans are for covered porches and patios that stay out of direct rain.
- **Wet-rated** fans can take direct rain and spray — pergolas, open patios and anywhere near a pool.

An indoor fan on a Texas porch won't last a season.

## Lights, controls and motors

Decide whether the fan needs a light kit — in a bedroom you may prefer separate lighting and a fan without one. Remote or wall controls are worth it on high ceilings, and DC motors run quieter and use less energy than older AC motors.
