Scale is the single most common thing people get wrong with lighting, and the dining room is where it shows the most. Here are the rules of thumb we use in the showroom.

## Size it to the table, not the room

For a fixture over a table, start with the table:

- **Round or square table:** choose a chandelier about **one-half to two-thirds the width of the table**. A 48" round table usually wants something around 24"–32" across.
- **Rectangular table:** a linear fixture about **two-thirds the length of the table** looks right, or use two matching pendants or chandeliers spaced evenly.
- **Leave elbow room:** keep the fixture at least **6" in from each edge** of the table so nobody bumps it when they stand up.

## A quick check for the room

If the room is open or the table isn't centered yet, a common shortcut is: **add the room's length and width in feet — that number, in inches, is a good starting diameter.** A 12' × 14' dining room adds to 26, so roughly a 26" fixture.

## How high to hang it

- With an **8-foot ceiling**, hang the bottom of the fixture about **30"–36" above the tabletop**.
- For **taller ceilings**, raise it about **3" for each additional foot** of ceiling height.
- Sit down before you commit. You want to see across the table under the fixture, and you don't want the bare bulbs in anyone's eyes.

## When to go bigger

Visual weight matters as much as size. An open, airy fixture can go larger than a solid drum or a heavy crystal piece. High ceilings, dark walls and big open-concept rooms can also take more fixture than the rules suggest. Most people who come back to us unhappy went too small, not too big.

## Put a dimmer on it

Whatever you choose, put the dining fixture on a dimmer. The same chandelier that's right for homework is too bright for dinner.
