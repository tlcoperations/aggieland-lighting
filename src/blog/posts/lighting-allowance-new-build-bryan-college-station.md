If you're building in Bryan–College Station, somebody has handed you a lighting allowance and probably not much else. This trips up almost everyone, so here's the plain version.

## What the allowance usually covers

Your allowance is a dollar figure your builder set aside for **light fixtures**. It usually covers the decorative pieces — the things you actually get to choose:

- Chandeliers in the dining room and entry
- Pendants over the kitchen island
- Vanity lights and bath sconces
- Ceiling fans
- Exterior and porch lights

## What it usually doesn't cover

Recessed cans, wiring, switches, dimmers and labor usually **don't** come out of the allowance. Those live in the electrical budget. Every builder writes it a little differently, so ask yours for the exact line item before you start shopping.

## Why it matters

The allowance is the fun part. It's also the part where the difference between a good decision and a rushed one shows up in every room for the next twenty years.

Most allowances are set before anyone has looked at a single fixture. Sometimes yours is generous. Sometimes it's going to be tight, and you'd rather find that out now than three weeks before drywall.

## How to make it go further

1. **Spend where people look.** The entry, the dining table and the kitchen island carry a house. Hallways, closets and secondary baths need fixtures that are right and quiet, not expensive.
2. **Get the scale right first.** A beautiful fixture that's too small looks cheap. See our guide on [how big a chandelier should be over a dining table](/blog/chandelier-size-over-dining-table/).
3. **Pick one color temperature for the whole house.** Mixing bulbs is the easiest way to make expensive fixtures look off. Read [2700K vs. 3000K](/blog/2700k-vs-3000k-color-temperature/).
4. **Watch lead times.** Some designer lines take weeks to arrive. Choose early so nothing holds up your builder.

## What to bring to the showroom

Your plans (printed or a PDF on your phone), your allowance number, ceiling heights if you know them, and photos of anything you've already picked — cabinets, tile, countertops, plumbing, flooring. Saved screenshots count. So does "I don't know, but not that."
